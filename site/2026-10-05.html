Emergency updates for an exploited NetScaler memory flaw lead the day, with active attempts against a Rejetto HFS session-forgery bug close behind, alongside a national population-register breach, an out-of-band Exchange fix, and an espionage campaign aimed at AI policy circles.

Actively exploited

Citrix has shipped emergency updates for CVE-2026-88779 Citrix patches NetScaler SAML zero-day exploited in attacks, a memory-buffer flaw in NetScaler ADC and Gateway that the supplied intelligence confirms is exploited as a zero-day and that sits in the CISA KEV catalog. NVD rates it CVSS 4.0 8.7 (High) — network vector, no privileges, no user interaction, but impact confined to availability, which fits the headline's framing as denial of service. The open question is whether it can also reach remote code execution; researchers are investigating, so treat that as unconfirmed. Affected branches include ADC before 14.1-73.41 and before 13.1-64.28, FIPS ADC before 14.1-73.41 and before 13.1-37.282, and Gateway before 14.1-73.41 and 13.1-64.28. CISA's required action references BOD 26-04 and asks agencies to weigh internet exposure, with a due date only days out; internet-facing appliances on those branches are the triage priority.

Second, CVE-2026-61500 Attackers Target Rejetto HFS Flaw That Enables Admin Session Forgery and RCE is session forgery in Rejetto HFS 3.0.0 through 3.2.0, rated CVSS 4.0 9.3 (Critical), CWE-338. HFS derives its session-cookie signing key from the non-cryptographic Math.random() generator and leaks outputs of that generator to unauthenticated clients during login: collect a few login responses, reconstruct the generator state, recover the key, forge an administrator cookie, and reach code execution through the server_code configuration feature. VulnCheck reports active exploitation attempts. KEV returned a confirmed catalog miss, which means only that — not that no one is exploiting it; the reported attempts rest on the story text.

Vulnerabilities

Microsoft issued out-of-band Exchange Server updates for CVE-2026-96940 Microsoft Exchange Flaw Lets Authenticated Attackers Read Other Users' Mailboxes, CVSS 3.1 8.8 (High), CWE-1390. The flaw is weak authorization that lets an authenticated attacker elevate privileges over the network, described in the excerpt as reading other users' mailboxes. Note the prerequisites: network vector, low privileges, no user interaction — a mailbox on the server is needed first. That places it below the two exploited bugs above, but it is a post-compromise escalation path worth prioritizing on on-premises fleets. No affected-version list was supplied. KEV returned a confirmed miss, and no in-the-wild exploitation is reported.

Research

A group tracked as TA419 built seemingly legitimate professional relationships with AI policy experts at US think tanks, universities and legal organizations Chinese Hackers Impersonate US Officials for AI Cyber Espionage. The supplied headline attributes the activity to Chinese hackers; the excerpt itself carries no technical attribution detail, so treat attribution as unconfirmed. Analysis: the notable element is targeting of the policy layer around AI rather than tooling — a template other intelligence and influence operations could copy.

Nozomi Networks reports exploitation attempts against a now-patched critical flaw in the Realtek Jungle SDK to deliver a botnet called Cling Realtek Jungle SDK Exploit Attempts Deliver Cling Botnet With STUN-Based C2. Cling's distinguishing feature is that it repurposes ordinary STUN behaviour as a command-and-control channel rather than inventing new propagation. No CVE identifier was supplied for the Realtek flaw, and the excerpt gives no victim counts or attribution.
