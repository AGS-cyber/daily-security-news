Today's briefing leads with a maximum-severity Oracle WebLogic vulnerability that CISA has added to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog following confirmed exploitation, plus a wave of Zimbra server compromises that has already passed 270 victims. Elsewhere, WordPress authentication bypasses are under active probing, a phishing-as-a-service operation has targeted thousands of Microsoft 365 tenants, and two malware families are positioning themselves as access brokers for ransomware gangs. The day also brings state-sponsored activity against critical infrastructure, a global law-enforcement sweep, and a reminder that security vendors themselves can fall to social engineering.

Actively exploited

The most urgent item is CVE-2026-21962, an improper access control flaw in Oracle HTTP Server and the Oracle WebLogic Server Proxy Plug-in that CISA has now confirmed as actively exploited Actively Exploited Oracle WebLogic Flaw Lets Unauthenticated Attackers Access Critical Data. The vulnerability carries a perfect CVSS score of 10.0 and requires no authentication, no user interaction, and only network access via HTTP. Successful exploitation gives an attacker unauthorized access to critical data and the ability to create, delete, or modify it, with a scope change meaning the impact can extend beyond the affected component. Affected versions include Oracle HTTP Server and the WebLogic Server Proxy Plug-in on 12.2.1.4.0, 14.1.1.0.0, and 14.1.2.0.0; for IIS deployments, only the 12.2.1.4.0 plug-in is vulnerable. CISA's guidance directs organizations to apply mitigations per vendor instructions, follow applicable guidance for cloud services, or discontinue use of the product if mitigations are unavailable. This is not an edge case: with exploitation already observed, the presumption should be that exposed instances are at immediate risk.

Meanwhile, attackers have already compromised more than 270 Zimbra Collaboration Suite servers in an ongoing remote code execution campaign Hackers breached over 270 Zimbra servers in ongoing attacks. The attacks exploit a high-severity ZCS vulnerability, and the scale of confirmed compromise suggests the flaw is being chained quickly. Organizations running Zimbra should treat this as an active incident, not a patch-cycle item. Exposed instances need immediate triage for signs of post-exploitation activity before remediation, because a patch applied after the fact does not undo an established foothold.

Vulnerabilities

WordPress sites using the miniOrange SAML 2.0 Single Sign On plugin are the target of active probing against two unauthenticated authentication bypasses Attackers Target miniOrange SAML Flaws That Can Grant WordPress Admin Access. One of them, CVE-2026-61979, is an unauthenticated privilege escalation flaw carrying a CVSS score of 8.1 and affecting plugin versions up to and including 5.4.3. The vulnerability allows an attacker to sign in as any WordPress user, including an administrator, which is effectively full site takeover without valid credentials. The fact that attackers are already probing these flaws suggests weaponization is likely imminent, and site operators using the plugin should treat this as a priority upgrade rather than a routine maintenance release.

Research

A long-running phishing-as-a-service campaign called Mirage2FA has hit roughly 4,500 companies across the US and EU, with Microsoft 365 accounts as the primary target Mirage2FA Surge Hits 4,500 US and EU Companies, Abusing Microsoft 365 Login Flows. Research by ANY.RUN shows the service abuses legitimate Microsoft login flows and is capable of bypassing two-factor authentication, undermining the defense that most organizations rely on to catch credential phishing. The campaign's effectiveness is stark: 48% of targeted email addresses were potentially compromised, and the victim list skews heavily toward US-based organizations. This is a reminder that attacker tooling has matured beyond simple credential harvesting and now commoditizes the bypass of standard account protections.

Two newly detailed malware families are also worth attention WordlistLoader Delivers Amatera via ClickFix, SynkLoader Phishes Windows Passwords. WordlistLoader is being distributed through ClearFake campaigns using ClickFix, the fake CAPTCHA technique that tricks users into running clipboard-pasted commands, and is used to deliver Amatera Stealer, also known as ACR Stealer or AcridRain Stealer. The second family, SynkLoader, takes a more direct approach by phishing Windows passwords from users. According to research from Gen Digital, both families appear designed to sell access to ransomware groups, meaning their infections are likely just the first stage of a more damaging incident. The combination of social engineering and loader infrastructure shows how initial access brokering continues to professionalize.

Industry

The US has sanctioned Iranian nationals over cyberattacks on critical infrastructure, acting shortly after reports emerged of an intrusion at a small power plant in the UK US sanctions Iranian cyber actors as UK discloses power plant attack. The pairing of financial pressure and disclosed incidents is notable less for the novelty of the tactics than for the target selection. Attacks on power generation facilities, even small ones, signal that state-aligned actors are willing to cross the threshold into operational technology environments. Sanctions may raise the cost of participation, but they also confirm what defensive teams already feared: critical infrastructure remains a live battlefield.

In a separate development, law enforcement agencies from 22 countries identified 263 suspects and arrested 58 individuals linked to cybercrime networks coordinated by African crime groups Police arrests dozens of suspects in global cybercrime crackdown. The operation is a meaningful step against the organized fraud ecosystems that have grown increasingly visible in global threat reporting. Arrests of this scale do not dismantle the entire economy, but they disrupt the coordinators and money movers who enable business email compromise and other scams. For defenders, the takeaway is that the human infrastructure behind these campaigns is becoming a target in its own right.

Breaches

ReliaQuest has confirmed that an intrusion by the ShinyHunters group began with a social engineering attack on a single employee ReliaQuest confirms failed data-theft attack after ShinyHunters breach. The attackers impersonated a member of the security team to gain the employee's trust, and although the company states the subsequent data-theft attempt failed, the breach is a useful case study in how even security vendors can be caught by the right pretext. ReliaQuest's business is protecting other organizations from precisely these tactics, and its own incident underscores that no amount of external vigilance eliminates the human factor. The lesson is not that security vendors are uniquely exposed; it is that every employee is a potential entry point, and the people most familiar with security operations may still be susceptible to a well-crafted impersonation.
