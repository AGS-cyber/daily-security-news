CISA confirmed active exploitation in two very different stacks — an AI/ML framework and a Windows host component — while researchers exposed a year-long portal scraping operation and two breaches underscored how much sensitive data still sits behind weak third-party controls.

Actively exploited

CISA's decision to add the Ray flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog is the clearest signal yet that attackers have moved beyond proof-of-concept to real-world use CISA Flags Actively Exploited Ray Flaw That Can Trigger Browser-Based RCE. The bug is a critical, browser-based remote code execution in Ray, the open-source, Python-native framework used to scale AI and machine learning workloads. Ray clusters increasingly sit on internet-reachable control planes, so a browser-triggered RCE can hand an attacker a foothold in the data-science stack — including the datasets and models the cluster exists to serve. The catalog entry cites active exploitation, making this an immediate patch-and-hunt item for any organization running Ray.

The second KEV addition is a reminder that older, already-flagged flaws do not fade; they get picked up by new actors. CISA has confirmed that ransomware gangs are now exploiting the Windows Task Host vulnerability it flagged as actively exploited earlier this year CISA: Windows Task Host flaw now exploited by ransomware gangs. The high-severity flaw sits in a Windows component many enterprises have not fully inventoried, and patching is no longer just hygiene — it is a direct step toward preventing a lockup event. Enterprises that deferred the fix should treat it as urgent, and those that already patched should still hunt for signs of compromise.

Vulnerabilities

Two critical, unauthenticated flaws demand web platform teams' attention. The wider-reaching is CVE-2026-15748, a 9.8-severity arbitrary file upload in the Forminator plugin for WordPress 300,000 WordPress Sites Potentially Exposed to Hacking Due to Form Plugin Flaw. The bug lives in the handle_file_upload function, where the dangerous-extension blocklist performs exact-key matching that attackers bypass via pipe-alternative MIME type keys; a public submission handler that trusts attacker-controlled upload configuration completes the chain. Unauthenticated attackers can upload executable files, and the plugin is present on more than 300,000 sites. The flaw is not currently listed in KEV, but a pre-authentication path to remote code execution across that many installs is effectively a mass-exploitation waiting room. Sites on versions up to and including 1.56.1 need the update immediately.

GitLab administrators have their own critical to process. CVE-2026-19478 is a 9.4-severity authentication bypass reachable through a GraphQL directive that lets an unauthenticated attacker remotely modify or delete public projects and user data Critical GitLab GraphQL Flaw Could Let Unauthenticated Attackers Delete Public Projects. The flaw affects Community and Enterprise Editions across the 18.2 through 19.2 branches, with fixed releases available in each line. Because GitLab often holds the canonical source and issue history for an entire engineering organization, an unauthenticated deletion can destroy the only copy of critical work. With no authentication required, this deserves to be patched ahead of routine maintenance.

Breaches

The breach at MyDr, a Polish supplier of software to doctors and clinics, may affect as many as 19 million people Poland probes MyDr healthcare software breach potentially affecting 19 million people. If that figure holds, it ranks among the largest healthcare-related exposures on record, and it blurs the line between a corporate incident and a public-health data event: MyDr's software sits between practitioners and their patients' records. The company says it identified and removed the cause and added extra security measures, but the harder work — determining exactly what was taken and who must be notified — is just beginning. Medical records carry downstream risk beyond identity theft, including targeted fraud and extortion.

A smaller but similarly sensitive incident is unfolding at Heights Finance, where a breach of a third-party platform exposed the information of at least 1.2 million individuals Heights Finance Data Breach Impacts at Least 1.2 Million Individuals. Names, addresses, phone numbers, Social Security numbers, and financial data give attackers everything needed for account takeover and synthetic identity fraud. The third-party detail matters: the data was held in a platform the lender relied on rather than its own infrastructure, which raises questions about how many other organizations share the same exposure.

Research

The most systemic story comes from research on the campaign dubbed City Forum One Attacker Has Scraped Both Salesforce and ServiceNow Portals Since 2025. A single attacker, operating from one server, has been scraping records out of Salesforce and ServiceNow customer portals across multiple industries for more than a year. The persistence and the singular infrastructure point to deliberate, methodical harvesting rather than opportunistic scanning. The underlying problem is architectural: portal misconfigurations and over-exposed record access keep offering the same class of sensitive data to anyone who knows where to look. SaaS security does not end with the provider's own safeguards; every customer must review who can read records through the portal and assume a determined actor will keep probing until the configuration is correct.

Industry

A Swiss ransomware trial puts a face on the international enforcement push. A 52-year-old Ukrainian software developer is accused of participating in an international ransomware operation that attacked Swiss train manufacturer Stadler Rail and other enterprises, and faces up to 12 years in prison Ukrainian software developer faces 12 years in Swiss ransomware trial. The case is notable less for technical novelty than for its demonstration that prosecution can follow across borders — an individual developer held to account in a foreign court for downstream damage. It is also a reminder that the people building and running these campaigns are increasingly identifiable, and that law enforcement pressure is becoming an active variable in the ransomware ecosystem.
