CISA added two entries to its exploited-vulnerability catalog — a critical Brotli decoder overflow that ships inside Node.js, .NET and Debian, and an Adobe Commerce/Magento flaw — while PolyShell storefront attacks, a chained PeopleSoft exploit and fresh Oracle E-Business Suite probing all compress the window between patch and intrusion.

Actively exploited

The Brotli entry is CVE-2026-111497: a CVSS 4.0 score of 9.2, rated Critical, buffer overflow in the decoder mapped to CWE-252 and CWE-787. A crafted Brotli-compressed stream from a remote, unauthenticated attacker can corrupt adjacent decoder state memory, disclose sensitive memory, cause denial of service or potentially execute arbitrary code. Libraries from 1.0.3 through 1.2.0 are affected; fixed releases are 1.0.10, 1.1.1 and 1.2.1, and the vulnerable code is nested inside Node.js, several .NET versions, the Python brotlicffi binding and Debian 12 — which is what makes a decoder CVE an inventory problem rather than a single upgrade. The second KEV addition covers Adobe Commerce and Magento, but the supplied material provides no CVE identifier for it; both were added citing evidence of active exploitation, alongside reporting of PolyShell attacks that breached hundreds of storefronts and of Brotli exploit traffic Adobe Commerce PolyShell Attacks Breach Hundreds of Stores as Brotli Exploit Traffic Emerges. CISA's remediation due date binds US federal agencies under BOD 26-04, not every estate; everyone else should treat KEV membership as a prioritisation signal, not a deadline.

Separately, researchers describe what appears to be an AI-assisted intrusion by a suspected China-nexus actor that combined PHP2Shell with a kernel privilege escalation to root 25 servers and reach a U.S. government-affiliated network PHP2Shell Attack Roots 25 Servers, Breaches U.S. Government-Affiliated Network. The attribution and the AI-assistance characterisation are both hedged in the source; what is concrete is the chain — web-facing flaw to root to adjacent high-value network.

The PeopleSoft bug ShinyHunters has been riding is not one flaw but three chained together, all patched by Oracle under a single identifier, CVE-2026-35273 ShinyHunters-Exploited Oracle PeopleSoft Zero-Day Turns Out to Be Three Chained Flaws. That CVE is a 9.8 Critical missing-authentication flaw in PeopleTools 8.61 and 8.62, unauthenticated over HTTP with full confidentiality, integrity and availability impact, in KEV since it was added with known ransomware campaign use recorded. The root cause is a server-side request forgery that reaches an internal management interface because PeopleSoft trusts its own loopback address. Analysis: that loopback-trust detail is the more durable takeaway — it is a hunting signature that survives the next single-identifier patch, though whether other deployments share the pattern is not established in the supplied material.

Vulnerabilities

Probing has begun for CVE-2026-101767, a 9.8 Critical flaw in the Oracle Application Object Library of E-Business Suite, days after Oracle's Critical Patch Update fixed it Hackers Target Critical Oracle E-Business Suite RCE Flaw Days After Security Patch. It affects 12.2.3 through 12.2.15 and allows an unauthenticated attacker with network access to take over the component. Scanning is not exploitation, and KEV enrichment returned a confirmed catalog miss — which does not establish that no exploitation has occurred. Internet-facing EBS teams have little room to stage the upgrade.

Browser teams get a large batch this cycle: Chrome 156 fixes 52 bugs and Firefox 159 carries 167 security patches, over 200 fixes in total Chrome and Firefox Updates Patch Over 200 Vulnerabilities. No individual CVEs were supplied, so the argument for acting is volume and reach rather than any named flaw.

Research

Volt Typhoon has been operating with a framework, "Silent Train," built on rotating chains of connected devices compromised on demand — including for zero-day attacks against high-value systems Volt Typhoon hides zero-day attacks using 'Silent Train' framework. The reframing is that this is reusable infrastructure rather than one-off intrusion tradecraft, which complicates the usual advice to hunt for a single actor's bespoke tooling.

Industry

The U.K. budget states the government will "consider where liability for cyber breaches should sit" — a potential break from the voluntary codes the country has relied on to push vendors toward better security UK to look at making software companies liable for cyber breaches. Nothing is committed yet; the significance is that vendor accountability now has a fiscal-process foothold.
