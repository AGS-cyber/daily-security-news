Actively exploited

Warning: Two Unpatched Citrix NetScaler RCE Zero-Days Under Active Exploitation is the one to act on first, and it is also the one where the usual playbook runs out. Two remote code execution flaws in Citrix NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway are being exploited in the wild, and there is no vendor confirmation, no advisory, and no CVE IDs to key your tracking on. Some administrators have concluded that waiting is not survivable and have taken internet-facing appliances offline entirely — a decision that costs availability but removes the exposure. If you run these at the edge, the question is not whether to patch but whether you can justify leaving them reachable while a fix does not exist.

Microsoft SharePoint Flaw CVE-2026-65660 Now Exploited in Attacks has the opposite problem: a fix exists, and the clock is short. CISA's KEV catalog now carries CVE-2026-65660, a code injection flaw in Microsoft SharePoint that allows an authenticated attacker with network access to execute code, scored 8.8 under CWE-94. The configuration data points at SharePoint Server subscription builds before 16.0.19725.20522, and at the 2016 and 2019 product lines. The federal remediation deadline falls only days after KEV addition, which tells you how the agency reads the risk; apply the vendor's mitigations, and do not treat that due date as a universal one, only as the federal bar. Note the privilege requirement: this is not pre-auth, so credential hygiene and MFA on SharePoint are load-bearing controls here, not nice-to-haves.

Then there is ShinyHunters uses WAF bypass trick in Oracle PeopleSoft attacks, which is a lesson about compensating controls rather than a new bug. CVE-2026-35273 in Oracle PeopleSoft Enterprise PeopleTools is a missing-authentication flaw in the Updates Environment Management component, rated 9.8 and affecting versions 8.61 and 8.62, with a long-standing KEV listing and catalog-noted ransomware campaign use. What is new is that ShinyHunters is bypassing WAF rules protecting it using a URL-encoding trick and resuming mass exploitation. Anyone who treated a WAF signature as the permanent answer to this flaw has an unauthenticated, ransomware-adjacent vulnerability still sitting on the internet.

Vulnerabilities

Cloudflare fixes Containers cross-tenant flaw exposing customer data is a fix, not a fire, but worth reading for the class of failure. Cloudflare patched a flaw in Containers and Sandboxes that let a customer on a Workers Paid account recover residual data belonging to other customers' containers on the same physical host. No CVE was assigned and the bug is closed, so the practical question is the design one: what "recovery" means for the data tenants leave behind when a container is torn down. Isolation failures of this kind rarely require an attacker to break in — they require only that one customer's cleanup is another customer's read.

Microsoft pauses KB5002907 update after Office license deactivations is the mirror image of a patch-cadence problem. Microsoft paused the KB5002907 Microsoft 365 update after it deactivated, and in some cases removed, perpetual Office 2016 and Office 2019 installations. Nothing here is a vulnerability, but the operational damage has the same shape as the outages admins spend patch cycles trying to avert, and it lands hardest on organisations still running perpetual licences in production. If you manage mixed Office estates, stage update channel changes deliberately rather than letting them arrive with the cycle.

Research

Lunex Stealer Abuses AMD Driver to Disable Security Monitoring and Steal Browser Credentials is the research read. Distribution through compromised Ukrainian websites, behind a ClickFix-style fake Cloudflare verification check, turns out to be part of a broader malware-as-a-service platform called Lunex. The chain runs four stages, and the interesting one abuses a signed AMD driver to blind endpoint monitoring before the payload goes after browser credentials — bring-your-own-vulnerable-driver, now reaching commodity stealer operations rather than bespoke intrusion sets. If your detection story rests on endpoint telemetry, assume the telemetry is the first thing removed; driver blocklisting and alerting on vulnerable-driver loads are controls that survive that assumption.

Industry

China and US Agree to Establish AI Safety Channel and Continue Trade and Military Talks is a policy story with a security edge. The US and China have agreed to establish a communication mechanism for artificial intelligence incidents, alongside continued trade and military talks — the first formal channel between the two governments for AI safety events. The significance is structural rather than immediate: AI incidents do not respect borders, and a channel that exists before a crisis is worth more than one negotiated during it. Whether it holds under pressure is a question only a real incident will answer.
