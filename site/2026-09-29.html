An out-of-bounds write in CoreGraphics is now confirmed exploited and sitting in CISA's catalog, ShinyHunters is turning an Oracle mitigation into an intrusion path, two NetScaler zero-days arrive simultaneously, and a Pentagon personnel agency has exposed records for three million people. The research queue is no quieter: a China-linked framework and a fresh Spectre variant both landed.

Actively exploited

The lead is the one most readers can act on without new tooling. Apple's CoreGraphics out-of-bounds write, tracked as CVE-2026-86950, is a CWE-787 write with a CVSS 3.1 base of 8.8 — the vector is network-reachable, low-complexity, no privileges, but requires user interaction, which is exactly what you'd expect from a flaw that triggers on processing a maliciously crafted file Apple Patches CoreGraphics Flaw Possibly Exploited in Targeted Attacks. That interaction requirement keeps it out of wormable territory, but it also means the exploitation surface is every file-parsing path on the device: mail attachments, downloads, previews, anything that hands a document to the graphics stack. The fix set is iOS 26.7.1, iPadOS 26.7.1, macOS Sequoia 15.8.1, and macOS Tahoe 26.7.1, and per the NVD-derived configuration data the vulnerable ranges are everything below those versions, with the Tahoe branch covering 26.0 up to but not including 26.7.1. Apple says it is aware of a report that the issue may have been exploited in an extremely sophisticated attack against specific targeted individuals, and CISA has added it to KEV, with a remediation requirement scoped to federal agencies under BOD 26-04 and a compressed deadline. Don't universalise that deadline, but do treat the KEV listing as the reason to stop treating this as a routine point release. The uncomfortable part for enterprise fleets is the "older versions" framing in the vendor advisory: the devices most likely to be stranded on the vulnerable builds are the ones nobody is managing.

The second exploited story is the one with no patch to point at. Researchers say ShinyHunters is exploiting workarounds for a flaw in a widely deployed Oracle product line, the same group that recently claimed credit for the intrusion at the FBI's jobs site ShinyHunters exploiting workarounds for Oracle PeopleSoft bug, Mandiant warns. Read the framing carefully: workarounds, not fixes. That usually means an internet-facing enterprise application — HR, finance, campus administration — where the vendor's guidance is a configuration change, and where the operational reality is that the system is three versions behind and owned by a business unit rather than a security team. There is no CVE supplied for this one, so there is no KEV entry and no NVD record to lean on; the intelligence here is adversary attribution and targeting, not vulnerability detail. For defenders, the actionable takeaway is the group, not the bug. ShinyHunters has demonstrated it will sit in enterprise SaaS and packaged applications and monetise the data rather than the encryption, and that changes what your detection programme should be looking at — bulk reads against personnel and finance tables, unusual API sessions, new OAuth grants — far more than it changes your patch queue.

Vulnerabilities

Two new critical zero-days in NetScaler products, both landing in default configurations, amount to a skeleton key into customer networks Dual NetScaler Zero-Days Trigger Chaos for Citrix Customers. The default-configuration detail is the part that should drive decisions: exposure is not limited to organisations that enabled an optional component or a non-standard listener, which removes the "that's not us" escape hatch that absorbs most of these announcements. If you run NetScaler, this is a weekend-shifting event, and the priority is inventory and exposure mapping before anything else.

The MikroTik story is a different shape of risk — not confirmed exploited, but a strong candidate for mass exploitation. CVE-2026-84411 carries a vendor CVSS of 9.8 across RouterOS before 7.24, an integer underflow that can yield remote code execution or denial of service MikroTik RouterOS. The flaw is not present in CISA's KEV catalog, and NVD enrichment is unavailable for it, so the vendor advisory is your source of truth — and a confirmed catalog miss is not evidence that nobody is trying. RouterOS sits on the edge at small and mid-sized organisations, ISPs, and industrial sites, which is precisely the population least likely to have a change-control process fast enough for a 9.8. Expect scanning within days and exploit code not long after.

Breaches

The larger of the two breaches is a federal one: records for three million people exposed at the Defense Manpower Data Center, which maintains personnel records across the Department of Defense Pentagon Personnel Agency Data Breach Impacts 3 Million People. Scale aside, the significance is the data class. Personnel files are the raw material for identity fraud, benefits fraud, and very effective social engineering against people with clearances, and the downstream harm curve runs for years. This is also a supply-chain story about consolidation — a single centre holding records for millions is a single point of failure with an unusually attractive payoff.

The more novel breach is small and, in the long run, more interesting. An automated AI agent breached the Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure, which described the incident as loud and very, very messy Automated AI agent used to breach cybersecurity nonprofit DIVD. A vulnerability-disclosure nonprofit is exactly the wrong-shaped target for a conventional criminal — there is no revenue there — which suggests the operator was testing capability rather than chasing data. Messy or not, this is an early real-world data point for agentic offensive operations: fast, automated, and indifferent to whether the target is worth robbing.

Research

Microsoft's writeup on NeedyMantis describes a previously unidentified malware framework used by a China-based actor in targeted intrusions, giving long-term access across telecommunications, universities, medical, and government-related organisations 'NeedyMantis' Provides Long-Term Access to Compromised Networks. The toolset being new matters more than the actor being known. Attribution is a comfortable frame; a fresh framework with a persistence story built into it means your existing indicators mostly don't apply, and the sectors named are the ones with long dwell times and complex supply relationships.

Finally, a reminder that hardware-level side channels are not a solved problem. A new Spectre v2 variant, Branch Target Reuse, recovers root password hashes on Intel systems running Linux in roughly three to five minutes on average New Spectre v2 attack variant leaks Linux root password hash in minutes. That figure is the headline. Defences against speculative-execution attacks across Intel, AMD, and Arm remain partial, and when a variant can extract credential material from kernel memory in minutes, the argument for treating side-channel mitigation as permanently unfinished — rather than as a task you close out — gets stronger.
