Actively exploited

Three new KEV entries landed, and only one should reorganise your queue: CVE-2026-20079, an authentication bypass in Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center, scored 10.0 on CVSS 3.1. The vector is maximally hostile — network reachable, no privileges, no user interaction, scope changed, full confidentiality, integrity and availability impact, and root on the underlying operating system as the outcome. NVD attributes it to CWE-288, an improper system process created at boot time, with an unauthenticated remote attacker sending crafted HTTP requests to execute scripts and commands CISA Flags Exploited Cisco, Citrix, Fortinet Flaws, Sets Sept. 12 Federal Patch Deadline. The KEV entry covers FMC alongside Security Cloud Control Firewall Management, with vulnerable configurations listed across the 7.0.x releases and into 7.1.0.x. Required action is mitigation per vendor instructions under BOD 26-04, on a federal remediation deadline set only days after the addition. That deadline binds FCEB agencies; for everyone else it is a signal about how confidently the bug is being used. FMC is management-plane infrastructure — root there is a path to every firewall it controls. The same round covers a Citrix flaw and a Fortinet flaw whose details aren't in front of us, but edge and management kit is where KEV entries keep clustering.

Microsoft's record Patch Tuesday is the counterexample to treating volume as urgency. Roughly 974 flaws, 723 in Windows, 111 in Office and Office 2016, 62 in SQL, 22 in Developer Tools, over 110 rated critical — but only two confirmed as exploited in the wild, both Windows zero-days Microsoft Patches Record 974 Flaws, Including Two Exploited Windows Zero-Days. The critical count tells you how big the release is; the exploited pair tells you what to do before the change window opens. Everything below that line is a capacity question, not an emergency.

Vulnerabilities

Against that backdrop, the reported ShieldCrash exploit against Microsoft Defender deserves attention out of proportion to its documentation. It is described as a zero-day yielding full SYSTEM privileges on Windows machines that are current on their patches, with no fix available New ‘ShieldCrash’ Zero-Day Exploit Targets Microsoft Defender. Two things follow. A fully patched endpoint is not a trusted endpoint, which is the uncomfortable premise of every Defender-adjacent attack path. And with no CVE and no vendor patch, mitigation is entirely environmental: shrink the exposed surface, watch for anomalous SYSTEM-level process and script activity on hosts that have no business producing it, and assume the bug is circulating quietly before it is named. Zero-days in security products are attractive precisely because defenders trust the agent.

Breaches

Two confirmations, both smaller in the telling than in the consequences. IDScan has confirmed attackers reached customer data in its cloud platform, days after reports tied it to a store of more than 153 million driver's license scans IDScan confirms breach tied to 153 million stolen driver’s licenses. The number is bad; the category is worse. Identity verification sits upstream of every other organisation's onboarding, and a licence scan cannot be reissued the way a password can. AdaptHealth confirmed 4.1 million people had health and insurance data exposed in an intrusion attributed to ShinyHunters AdaptHealth confirms 4.1 million people exposed in July cyberattack — another entry in the long pattern where the intrusion is discovered well before the population is told. For both, the exploitable interval runs from compromise, not disclosure, and neither dataset improves with age.

Industry

The Justice Department's action against Xinbi Guarantee is the most structurally interesting enforcement story in a while: Telegram channels seized, two cryptocurrency wallets taken, $52.8 million frozen, and the Scam Center Strike Force deployed to Madagascar against 13 compounds run by Chinese organised crime U.S. Disrupts Xinbi Guarantee Scam Marketplace, Freezes $52.8 Million in Crypto. Hitting the marketplace rather than the compounds targets the trust layer — the guarantee brand is what lets otherwise hostile operations transact — and freezing the crypto is the part that actually costs money. Separately, the EU's Cyber Resilience Act reporting duty takes effect imminently, giving organisations 24 hours to notify serious product security incidents EU Cyber Resilience Act to Enforce New Reporting Requirements. A day is not much time to establish scope, but it is solvable if disclosure is a rehearsed process rather than a scramble. Most programmes will find out that it isn't.

Research

BlueMoon is the story that should shape your detection roadmap. The exploit kit chains multiple Windows and Chrome vulnerabilities, and its first in-the-wild use has been attributed to the China-aligned group tracked as APT31 — but within a week, four separate espionage-motivated clusters were using it Four Spy Groups Used the Same Chrome and Windows Exploit Kit Within a Week. Fresh exploit capability is now a commodity that gets resold, leaked or copied faster than the underlying components can be patched individually. The takeaway isn't "patch Chrome faster" so much as "detect the chain": the value of a kit is in the sequence, and interrupting it anywhere defeats every group downstream of the original author.
