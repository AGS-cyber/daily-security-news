Today’s briefing is defined by one unambiguous priority: an actively exploited critical Gitea vulnerability has entered CISA’s known-exploited catalog, and every exposed instance should be treated as compromised until patched. That story anchors the day, but it is far from the only one worth your attention. Chrome shipped one of its largest patch waves ever, researchers exposed a backdoor that hides in memory until a single packet arrives, and international police action against West African cybercrime produced a major haul of arrests. Meanwhile, the healthcare sector adds another breach, an iPhone-focused phishing service commercializes passcode theft, and a pair of WordPress authentication bypasses create urgent risk for enterprise SSO deployments.

Actively exploited

Attackers are actively exploiting CVE-2026-60004, a critical remote code execution vulnerability in Gitea Critical Gitea RCE Actively Exploited as Reported Attack Drops Miner-Like Payload. The flaw carries a CVSS score of 9.8 and allows any user with ordinary write access to a repository to execute arbitrary shell commands on the underlying host. CISA has confirmed the vulnerability is under active exploitation and added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, describing it as a code injection issue and assigning a required action with a short compliance deadline for federal agencies. For everyone else, the calculus is no different in practice: this is a confirmed, in-the-wild RCE in a widely deployed self-hosted Git platform, so the vendor’s patched versions should be applied immediately. Even if the exposed surface appears limited to authenticated users, the write-access precondition is often satisfied in real-world collaboration workflows, and the attacker behavior observed in the wild makes this a worst-case scenario for unpatched deployments.

Industry

CISA is warning that more than 100 internet-exposed water systems were targeted in a coordinated wave of attacks, with the agency tying the activity to Iran-linked hackers CISA: Over 100 Internet-Exposed Water Systems Targeted in July Cyberattacks. The guidance is blunt: critical infrastructure providers should eliminate unnecessary internet exposure rather than rely on perimeter defenses that are demonstrably failing. Water and wastewater systems have long been a soft target, and this campaign shows the threat is not theoretical. Operators should audit their OT and IT boundaries for unintended public-facing interfaces, apply vendor hardening guidance, and assume that visibility into these systems is an attacker priority.

On the enforcement side, INTERPOL’s Operation Jackal IV concluded an eight-month, 22-country push against West African organized crime, resulting in 58 arrests and the identification of 263 suspects INTERPOL Operation Jackal IV Arrests 58, Identifies 263 in Global Cyber Fraud Crackdown. The operation specifically targeted networks such as Black Axe and affiliated groups that have become central players in global business-email compromise, romance scams, and phishing. The scale of this takedown is a useful reminder that the threat from these groups is not confined to one region; they operate across continents, and disrupting them requires the kind of sustained international coordination that this operation represents.

Vulnerabilities

Chrome 152 patches more than 300 vulnerabilities, a number that stands out even for a browser with Chrome’s attack surface Chrome 152 Patches Over 300 Vulnerabilities. A substantial portion of the fixes came from Google’s own AI-driven discovery efforts, which is a welcome development in the race to find bugs before attackers do. The release is also a reminder that Chrome remains a high-value target, and the sheer volume of patches means organizations should treat this update as a priority. The fact that researchers are still independently finding high-severity flaws in Chromium suggests the browser will stay a hot spot for exploit development.

WordPress sites using the MiniOrange SAML Single Sign On plugin are facing two distinct authentication issues WordPress Websites Targeted via MiniOrange Plugin Vulnerabilities. The more severe, CVE-2026-15981, is an unauthenticated authentication bypass rated 9.8 due to a loose boolean check on OpenSSL’s signature verification. A crafted SAMLResponse that triggers an OpenSSL error is treated as having a valid signature, allowing an attacker to log in as any WordPress user, including an administrator. The second flaw, CVE-2026-61979, is an unauthenticated privilege escalation rated 8.1 affecting earlier plugin versions. Given how commonly the MiniOrange SAML plugin is used in enterprise and government WordPress deployments, these are not just blog-park bugs; they are plausible entry points for full site compromise. Affected sites should move to patched versions without delay and audit for unexpected new admin accounts.

Breaches

Nutex Health has confirmed that it detected unauthorized access and data exfiltration, disclosing the incident to the SEC Sensitive Information Exposed in Nutex Health Data Breach. The healthcare sector continues to be one of the most attractive targets for attackers, and this breach fits that pattern. While technical details remain thin, the confirmation of exfiltration means organizations should expect downstream effects, including potential regulatory scrutiny and fallout from stolen sensitive information. For security teams, the practical takeaway is that healthcare providers should assume breach-magnitude impacts and prioritize data-centric controls, not just network perimeter monitoring.

Research

Independent research has documented a previously unreported Windows backdoor called SLEEPWALKER that stays fully inert in memory until a specially crafted network packet reaches the machine Newly SLEEPWALKER Backdoor Waits for One Crafted Packet, Then Runs Its Own Bytecode. At that point, it executes commands written in a bespoke 23-instruction bytecode language of its own design. The sample is an unsigned 64-bit DLL built for side-loading, and the combination of dormancy and custom bytecode is a clever evasion technique designed to frustrate both dynamic analysis and signature-based detection. This is the kind of research that deserves attention because it shows attackers are willing to build bespoke, hard-to-analyze tooling for what appears to be targeted deployments.

Separately, researchers have detailed AnonyMousKIT, a phishing-as-a-service platform built to strip Apple’s Activation Lock from stolen iPhones Fake Apple Support AI Calls Target Stolen-Device Owners for Passcodes and 2FA Codes. The service uses rented AI voice agents that call victims, impersonate Apple Support, and request the device passcode and 2FA codes. It is credit-metered, meaning users pay per completed call, and it drives lures across multiple channels. This turns a highly social-engineering-dependent crime into a scalable commercial operation, and it represents a meaningful evolution in the anti-theft bypass economy. For defenders, the takeaway is that voice-based phishing is no longer a human-only threat; AI-driven phone calls are now a commodity service that can be aimed at precisely the people most likely to panic and comply.
