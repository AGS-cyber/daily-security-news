Attackers have started exploiting a critical remote code execution flaw in Zimbra Collaboration Suite, according to a warning from CERT Polska Critical Zimbra RCE flaw now actively exploited in attacks. Zimbra servers sit at the email perimeter and often hold years of credentials and sensitive correspondence, making this a patch-now rather than patch-soon situation. Organizations running ZCS should check for exposed admin interfaces, review mailbox access logs for anomalies, and apply the vendor's update without waiting for a maintenance window. The criticality of the issue, combined with confirmed exploitation, puts this at the top of the queue.

The picture is even sharper for GitLab: CVE-2026-19478 is already being exploited in the wild within days of disclosure Critical GitLab Flaw Exploited Shortly After Disclosure. The NVD record rates it 9.4 out of 10 and associates it with CWE-94; the flaw lets an unauthenticated attacker modify or delete public projects and user data through a GraphQL directive. Fixed versions are 18.11.11, 19.0.8, 19.1.6, and 19.2.4. The CVE is not listed in CISA's KEV catalog, but that absence is a catalog miss, not evidence that exploitation is not happening. GitLab admins should upgrade immediately, audit projects for unauthorized changes, and watch for account activity tied to GraphQL endpoints.

The same urgency extends to MLflow. CISA says threat actors are actively exploiting a critical flaw in the open-source AI engineering platform and has warned federal agencies to address it CISA warns of hackers exploiting critical MLflow vulnerability. MLflow deployments are often internal and may have weak access controls, so teams should inventory every instance, check whether management interfaces are reachable from untrusted networks, and apply the vendor's fixes or mitigations. The broader lesson is that email and AI infrastructure are being attacked at the same time; a response plan that prioritizes one at the expense of the other is incomplete.

Industry

On the critical infrastructure side, U.S. agencies have published an advisory describing an active threat to Siemens S7 Series PLCs, and they stress that the targeting is broader than Siemens alone Defending Against an Active Threat to Siemens S7 Series PLCs. The warning describes AI-assisted attacks on programmable logic controllers and urges all PLC owners and operators to apply mitigations. For operational technology teams, the actionable takeaway is to treat every internet-facing or weakly segmented PLC as a candidate target, review remote access rules, and verify the integrity of control logic. The Siemens-specific guidance is one piece of a wider defensive posture, not a narrow product bulletin.

Separately, the U.S. has charged 17 Iranians, alleged members of the hacking-for-hire company Mabna Institute, over a years-long campaign that stole intellectual property from American organizations with losses valued at $3.4 billion US charges Iranian hackers over $3.4 billion intellectual property theft. The charges may not recover the data, but they provide a public record that can help defenders understand the threat model.

Breaches

The healthcare sector absorbed another large breach notification: CareCloud says 3,756,469 people had information leaked after a hacker spent eight hours inside one of its electronic health record environments Electronic health record company CareCloud says 3.7 million people affected by breach. The filing with the Department of Health and Human Services confirms the scale, and the nature of the data suggests a high regulatory and reputational cost. Healthcare organizations that use CareCloud should treat the incident as a supply-chain signal: ask for details on the affected environment, change shared credentials, and review third-party access logs. For everyone else, the event is a reminder that EHR platforms concentrate sensitive data in ways that make them attractive to attackers.

Research

Finally, researchers have demonstrated that a remote Spectre attack can leak a JSON Web Token from a co-located Worker in Cloudflare's production environment at up to 12 bits per second, roughly 360 times faster than an earlier demonstration Cloudflare Workers Spectre Attack Leaks JWT From Co-Located Worker at 12 Bits/Second. The experiment used an attacker Worker and a victim Worker both controlled by the researchers, so it is not evidence of a malicious tenant on Cloudflare's network. It is, however, a concrete demonstration that serverless isolation assumptions are not absolute and that transient-execution attacks remain relevant outside a local CPU context. For security teams, the practical implications are to avoid placing highly sensitive data or long-lived tokens in workloads co-located with untrusted code, and to watch for vendor isolation updates.
