Actively exploited

The lead is a speed problem, not a novelty problem. Attackers began targeting Attackers Target Critical Atlassian Vulnerability Within Hours of PoC Publication, an arbitrary file access flaw in Atlassian's self-hosted Data Center line, within hours of a public proof-of-concept. The NVD description puts CVE-2026-21589 at CVSS 4.0 9.3 (Critical), CWE-552, reachable by an unauthenticated remote attacker — but with a real constraint: exploitation requires knowing the exact name and path of the target file, and the flaw does not permit directory enumeration. Some configurations will hold sensitive files in the web application root, which is where the severity bites. Affected products named in the supplied description span Bitbucket, Confluence, Jira Service Management, Jira Software, Bamboo, Crowd, Crucible and Fisheye Data Center, with fix versions listed for several. CISA KEV shows a confirmed catalog miss for this CVE; that is a catalog fact, not evidence that exploitation is absent — and the story text reports live targeting.

Two Zammad flaws have the stronger formal signal. CISA added CVE-2026-102489 and CVE-2026-102490 to KEV on evidence of active exploitation, per CISA Adds Two Known Exploited Vulnerabilities to Catalog. Both are rated CVSS 4.0 9.4 (Critical). The first is a session-fixation bug (CWE-384) leading to remote code execution as the zammad user in versions 6.3.0 through 6.5.4; the supplied description notes the bug is present in 7.0.0–7.1.2 but not exploitable there due to framework changes. The second is improper privilege management (CWE-269), letting the local zammad user escalate to root — described as affecting all versions including the latest alpha. CISA's required action references vendor instructions and BOD 26-04, with a federal remediation due date of 2026-10-05; that date binds federal agencies, not everyone. Known ransomware campaign use is listed as unknown. Analysis: the pairing is notable — RCE as the application user plus a local path to root — but the supplied vectors show user interaction as passive (UI:P) and privilege requirements of none, which does not establish that either bug is remotely triggerable end-to-end without a foothold. Post-compromise escalation is the more defensible reading of the second.

FBI: Ongoing FortiBleed attacks lock out FortiGate VPN admins adds an operational warning rather than a new CVE: the FBI says FortiBleed attacks are ongoing against exposed FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPN gateways, with attackers locking out legitimate administrators. No CVE identifiers, versions or remediation steps were supplied with this item, so treat the lockout behaviour as the actionable detail and do not map it to a specific advisory on the strength of the name alone.

Breaches

The scale revision is the story in Oracle Health Data Breach Tally Climbs to Nearly 20 Million: the Oracle Health breach tally has climbed to nearly 20 million people, well above the counts in earlier filings and patient notifications. Revised counts usually reflect completed matching rather than a new intrusion, but the supplied evidence does not say, so the mechanism behind the jump remains unconfirmed.

Hackers hijack Google domains after breaching ccTLD registries is the more structurally interesting one. Attackers compromised third-party country-code top-level domain registry operators, modified authoritative DNS records, obtained unauthorised HTTPS certificates for several Google domains and hijacked names under Ghana, American Samoa and Sierra Leone. The significance is that every name under those ccTLDs depends on the registry's integrity — certificate issuance and DNS authority failed together, which is the combination that makes hijacks convincing.

In Leaked chats show Russian extortion gang sending ‘agents’ into US law firms, leaked chats describe a Russian extortion operation that tracked dozens of victims, haggled over multimillion-dollar payments and directed operatives based in the United States it calls "agents," targeting law firms. The evidence is the chat record; attributions of nationality and structure come from that material and are reported as such.

Research

FakeGit malware campaign returns with 17,610 malicious GitHub repos is a distribution-channel reminder: the reactivated FakeGit campaign has seeded more than 17,610 malicious GitHub repositories pushing SmartLoader and the StealC infostealer. Developer platforms remain a live channel because the trust signal is the repository, not the malware. Analysis: the campaign's scale, not its tooling, is what raises the odds of an employee cloning the wrong dependency.
