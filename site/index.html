Two additions to the federal known-exploited catalog set the day's priorities — one of them a local privilege escalation rather than a remote entry point — while confirmed compromises at an AI coding marketplace, a hardware wallet reseller and a Japanese city show trust in the supply chain being spent in three different ways.

Actively exploited

CISA added two vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on evidence of active exploitation CISA Adds Two Known Exploited Vulnerabilities to Catalog. CVE-2026-114713 is an improper neutralization of delimiters flaw in UnrealIRCd; no NVD enrichment was supplied, so its severity and affected versions are unknown here. CVE-2026-123039 is a Check Point Multiple Products incorrect permission assignment issue (CWE-732, with CWE-862 also listed), rated 7.8 High on CVSS 3.1 with a local vector (AV:L, PR:L, UI:N). Per the supplied description, the Gaia OS DynamicCLI daemon runs as root and listens on a world-writable local socket without authenticating connecting processes, letting an authenticated local user have a command executed as root. Analysis: that makes the Check Point entry a post-access escalation path, so the exposure to check first is hosts already reachable by low-privileged or compromised accounts — the ranking reflects confirmed exploitation, not a remote-attack claim. Both carry the same short federal remediation deadline under BOD 26-04; that clock binds federal agencies, not every operator. Ransomware use is listed as unknown for both.

Breaches

Cursor confirmed a breach that let attackers distribute a malicious Claude Code extension through its marketplace after stealing credentials from AI infrastructure provider Fireworks AI Cursor confirms security breach led to malicious Claude Code extension. Analysis: the pivot is the story — an extension marketplace inherits the trust of the AI provider behind it, so credential theft one hop away becomes code running in customers' editors. Affected versions and remediation were not supplied.

Trezor confirmed that three Safe 5 wallets sold by Southeast Asian reseller CryptoBilis contained malicious hardware implants designed to steal recovery phrases and send them to attackers over cellular networks, and says it has released a fix Trezor confirms crypto-stealing implants in three wallets, releases fix. Analysis: the implant defeats the assumption that the device is the trusted end of the chain, making reseller handling part of the threat model. Fix details were not supplied.

Osaka warned of a data breach impacting roughly a million people and told its 2.8 million residents to be wary of suspicious emails purporting to come from the local government, as Japan contends with dozens of cyber incidents City of Osaka warns of data breach impacting 1 million as Japan reels from dozens of cyber incidents. The follow-on phishing warning is the concrete near-term risk to residents.

Industry

The U.S. government disrupted a North Korean social engineering campaign called "FaceStealer," responsible for stealing more than $400 million in cryptocurrency US disrupts North Korean campaign behind $400 million crypto theft. Analysis: the supplied basis is the campaign's recruitment-style social engineering, which is the tradecraft worth writing detections against; the disruption's effect on ongoing operations is not established.

Officials say there is no evidence of a cyberattack behind the nationwide FAA ground stop, which may have been caused by a software update that the FBI is reportedly examining No Signs of Hacking in FAA Ground Stop as FBI Reportedly Probes Software Update. Analysis: a useful case study in mundane update pipelines becoming critical-infrastructure risk, but the cause remains unconfirmed.

Analysis: treat the three-hour figure as a detection benchmark — the signal is chained technique velocity, not any single exploit.
