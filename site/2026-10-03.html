Standfirst: Ransomware delivery through SharePoint, an MI5 warning on state-funded academics, a maximum-scoring Dell flaw, two institutional breaches and a newly documented China-nexus backdoor.

Actively exploited

Warlock, described as a suspected China-linked actor, is still weaponising Microsoft SharePoint vulnerabilities, "likely both old and new," against organisations in Portuguese- and Spanish-speaking countries, with victims spanning critical infrastructure, government and education Warlock Exploits SharePoint Flaws to Disable Security Tools and Deploy Ransomware. No CVE identifiers were supplied for the SharePoint flaws, so the specific defects in play cannot be confirmed from this evidence. Analysis: reaching an existing access vector, disabling security tooling and then deploying ransomware points to tooling reuse rather than a new exploit chain, but the supplied excerpt is truncated and does not describe the disablement technique.

Industry

An MI5 espionage alert states that more than 100 academics have helped China's intelligence gathering on behalf of its state security service, naming the China General Technology Research Institute as funding research whose primary purpose was that effort MI5 Says China’s MSS Funded Research Involving 100+ U.K.-Linked Academics. The scale of the figure is the story. Analysis: naming both an institute and a funding mechanism reads as pressure on universities' collaboration and disclosure decisions; the supplied excerpt gives no individual cases or consequences.

Vulnerabilities

Dell has released updates for multiple critical flaws in its Container Storage Modules. One, CVE-2026-63688, carries a CVSS score of 10.0 — a missing authentication for critical function issue in the csm-authorization-storage gRPC server Dell CSM Flaws Enable Unauthenticated Admin Access and Root on Kubernetes Nodes. On the supplied data it is a confirmed CISA KEV miss, which establishes only that it is not catalogued, not that exploitation has not occurred. The other flaws in the set were not itemised here.

GitLab's AI Gateway flaw rates 9.9 and requires a logged-in user with Duo Agent Platform access, with command execution possible "under certain conditions" — an authenticated path, not pre-auth. Only organisations self-hosting the gateway need to act, and fixes are in gateway versions 19.2.4, 19.3.2 and 19.4.1 GitLab Patches Critical 9.9 AI Gateway Flaw Allowing Command Execution on Self-Hosted Servers. No CVE identifier was supplied. Analysis: the authentication prerequisite limits practical exposure, while the near-maximum score reflects the gateway's position in the AI trust path.

Breaches

The Technical University of Denmark says information on up to 200,000 users may have been exposed after attackers reached its identity and access management system and downloaded a large amount of data Danish university DTU breach exposes data of up to 200,000 people. Frontline Education is notifying school districts after attackers exploited a vulnerability in third-party software to steal employee information including Social Security numbers Frontline Education breach exposes school district employee data. Analysis: in both, initial access sat with a system other than the records themselves — identity infrastructure at one, an unnamed third-party product at the other.

Research

Cisco Talos reports a China-nexus cluster deploying a previously undocumented backdoor, Antino, against government and policy organisations in Taiwan, India, the Philippines, Cambodia, Pakistan, Thailand and Myanmar, using Outlook and OneDrive for command-and-control Antino Backdoor Uses Outlook and OneDrive for C2 in China-Nexus Espionage Campaign.
