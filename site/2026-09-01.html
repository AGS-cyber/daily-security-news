The day’s most urgent news is concentrated in active exploitation: CISA has added two PaperCut flaws to its known-exploited catalog, JFrog Artifactory and a pair of critical application bugs are under attack, and defenders are also juggling a nine-figure health data breach, thousands of exposed Exchange servers, critical firewall patches, and a new social-engineering campaign that installs reverse tunnels. For enterprise teams, the through line is patch velocity: the vulnerabilities being exploited now include print-management servers, DevOps repositories, and AI workflow tooling, expanding the attack surface well beyond the usual suspects.

Actively exploited

PaperCut’s print-management servers have moved from “patch soon” to “patch now.” CISA has added both CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078 to its known-exploited catalog, and the agency’s designation reflects active intrusions rather than theoretical risk PaperCut Exploitation Escalates to Active Intrusions. The first is an access-control flaw in the web management interface that lets unauthenticated remote requests modify system configuration before validation completes; the second is an unsafe dynamic class-loading issue in database connection utilities that can lead to arbitrary Java bytecode execution. Together they put internet-exposed PaperCut NG and MF installations in a dangerous position. Patched releases are identified in the 24.1.9, 25.0.12, and 26.0.4 lines, and while CISA’s requirement for federal agencies includes a specific remediation deadline, every organization running the affected versions should treat the active exploitation as a real incident trigger. The 9.4 CVSS score on the reflection flaw understates the practical risk because it assumes some configuration control; the login bypass is the part that makes that assumption less comforting.

Also under active attack is the critical authentication bypass in JFrog Artifactory, CVE-2026-82329 Critical JFrog Artifactory Vulnerability Reportedly Exploited in the Wild. The flaw, rated 9.8, allows an unauthenticated attacker to gain administrative privileges under default configuration. What makes this stand out is speed: exploitation reportedly started within days of disclosure, and Artifactory is widely used to store and distribute software artifacts, so full administrative compromise is not just a repository problem but a supply-chain trust problem. It has no KEV entry at this point, but the observed attacks make it a priority regardless.

A broader credential-probing and command-and-control effort is abusing two more critical bugs, according to new research Attackers Exploit Critical Langflow and Rails Flaws in Credential-Probing and C2 Activity. CVE-2026-0768 in Langflow is a code-injection flaw in the validate endpoint that requires no authentication and executes Python as root, with a 9.8 CVSS score. In Ruby on Rails, CVE-2026-66066 is an Active Storage issue where unsafe libvips operations are not disabled for untrusted uploads, allowing an unauthenticated attacker to read arbitrary files accessible to the Rails process, including secrets and environment variables. The Rails advisory lists fixed versions in the 7.2.3.2, 8.0.5.1, and 8.1.3.1 trains. The fact that these two very different stacks are being hit at the same time is a reminder that attackers are systematically scanning for any critical authentication or input-handling flaw, rather than concentrating on a single vendor.

Breaches

Healthcare remains a top target for data exfiltration. The Aesto Health breach hit the company’s AWS infrastructure, with attackers stealing personal and health information belonging to 9.5 million people 9.5 Million Impacted by Aesto Health Data Breach. The scale puts it among the largest health-sector incidents in recent memory, and the location of the data in cloud storage rather than legacy on-prem systems complicates forensics and notification. For security teams in healthcare and adjacent industries, this is another sign that cloud misconfigurations and compromised cloud credentials are an efficient path to mass data theft.

Vulnerabilities

The exposure problem is not always a zero-day. Nearly 22,000 internet-reachable Microsoft Exchange servers remain unpatched against a high-severity authentication bypass that can let an attacker hijack every mailbox on the server Nearly 22,000 Microsoft Exchange servers vulnerable to hijack attacks. Exchange’s history of mass-exploited vulnerabilities means these unpatched servers are a standing invitation. The fix has been available, yet the exposure persists, suggesting asset owners are not treating Exchange as a product that requires aggressive lifecycle management.

On the positive side, WatchGuard has shipped patches for three critical remote code execution vulnerabilities in the iked process of Fireware OS, all exploitable by unauthenticated attackers WatchGuard Patches Critical Vulnerabilities. Firewalls are a natural target because they sit at the boundary between untrusted traffic and internal networks, and a successful remote code execution there gives attackers the same access as a VPN compromise. The patch should be treated as urgent, and not just for the firewall itself; the same appliance often controls tunnels into the rest of the environment.

Research

A new campaign tracked as TerminalFix is giving the ClickFix trick a stronger foothold. Attackers are compromising legitimate websites and using fake Cloudflare CAPTCHA prompts to convince users to paste malicious PowerShell commands into Windows Terminal Microsoft warns of TerminalFix attacks deploying reverse tunnels. Microsoft is warning of enterprise intrusions where the payloads deploy reverse tunnels, effectively punching a controlled outbound hole through perimeter defenses. This is a meaningful evolution of the social-engineering playbook because the prompt is designed to feel like an identity check, and the use of Windows Terminal makes the malicious step look like a normal response to a security prompt.

Industry

Finally, the Financial Stability Board is pushing financial institutions to treat frontier AI as a systemic cyber risk. In a report framed by the board’s chair, the most immediate concern is not a single AI-powered attack but the concentration and correlation of AI dependence across the global financial system Cyber risk from frontier AI poses ‘most immediate concern’ to global financial system, watchdog warns. The call for institutions and technology providers to prepare for severe scenarios involving simultaneous disruption across multiple firms or shared technology dependencies points to a growing recognition that resilience planning needs to account for common modes of failure, not just per-entity risk.
