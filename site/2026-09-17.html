Actively exploited

CVE-2026-76460 in Cisco Identity Services Engine is the day's least deferrable item. It scores 10.0, it's KEV-listed with confirmed exploitation, and the mechanism is as bad as the number suggests: CWE-648, incorrect use of privileged APIs, meaning an API endpoint fails to enforce authentication properly. A single crafted request, no credentials and no user interaction, bypasses the web-based management interface entirely — remote, network-reachable, with scope changed across confidentiality, integrity and availability Cisco Warns of New Zero-Day ISE Auth Bypass (CVSS 10.0) Exploited in Active Attacks. ISE is where network access control, posture assessment and identity policy live, so a bypass is not one compromised appliance; it is a foothold with a view of who and what is allowed on the network. NVD's configuration data marks vulnerable builds across both the 3.1.0 and 3.2.0 trees, patches included, so "we're on a current patch level for our branch" is not an answer. CISA's required action directs federal agencies to apply vendor mitigations under its risk-based patching guidance, with a due date that governs those agencies rather than everyone — but a 10.0 with confirmed in-the-wild exploitation is an out-of-band change window whatever your compliance regime says.

The second exploited flaw is a reminder that severity scores understate blast radius. CVE-2026-87886 in Acronis Backup for cPanel and WHM is only a 7.8 — local privilege escalation from insecure default file permissions — but it is KEV-listed, confirmed exploited, and sits on shared hosting control panels where one local escalation crosses every tenant on the box Acronis cPanel Backup Plugin Vulnerability Exploited in Targeted Attacks. Named for incorrect default permissions, this is not a subtle bug requiring unusual configuration; it is the kind of thing that gets swept up in post-exploitation playbooks. If you run cPanel/WHM at any scale, treat this as an inventory problem before a patching problem: you cannot patch plugins you don't know are installed.

Then there is Issabel. CVE-2026-89026 is a hard-coded HS256 JWT signing key in the pbxapi index.php file, identical across every installation, so anyone can mint a valid bearer token, call the manager originate endpoint with the System application parameter, and get Asterisk to execute arbitrary OS commands as the Asterisk user Attackers Exploit Issabel Framework Flaw Enabling Unauthenticated OS Command Execution. It scores 9.8 under CVSS v3.1 and 9.3 under v4.0, exploitation has been observed in the wild, and the fix is a commit rather than a version bump — meaning release-note-driven patch programs will miss it. The interesting datapoint is the catalog: this CVE has a confirmed KEV miss despite observed exploitation. KEV remains a floor for prioritisation, not a ceiling, and this is the case to cite next time someone argues that only catalogued bugs get worked.

Vulnerabilities

Unbound carries a critical heap overflow in its DNSSEC validator, CWE-122, affecting every release up to and including 1.26.0 and fixed in 1.26.1 Critical Unbound DNSSEC Validator Flaw Could Allow RCE via a Malicious DNS Zone. The trigger is elegant: a DNSKEY with an owner compression pointer into its own RDATA overflows the digest buffer, and since the digested data is attacker-controlled, remote code execution — not just the denial of service you'd first assume — is on the table. CVSS v4.0 puts it at 9.1. No exploitation has been reported and there's no KEV entry, but neither fact is reassurance: a recursive resolver queries hostile zones constantly as a normal part of its job, which means the precondition of "attacker controls a zone" is satisfied by default for anyone on the internet. Check what's serving DNS recursion inside your perimeter and in your container platforms, not just your managed resolvers.

Breaches

The Gyazo disclosure is a credential event disguised as an image-hosting incident: roughly 23.62 million user records including email addresses and password hashes, plus about 490 million image metadata records — largely historical uploads rather than recent ones — containing the IDs that make up Gyazo image links Gyazo Breach Exposes 23.62 Million User Records and 490 Million Image Metadata Records. The hashes are the immediate operational problem: reset, monitor for reuse, and expect stuffing attempts against unrelated services. The metadata is the slower one — link IDs enable correlation and enumeration of years-old content that users assumed was either gone or unguessable.

Revolut's case is the opposite shape and more instructive. Customer information was allegedly handed to attackers impersonating an Italian government agency over roughly five months, touching 680 high-profile accounts, with a $3M ransom demand attached Revolut Data Breach: 5 Months, 680 High-Profile Accounts, $3M Ransom. Five months is not a control failure, it is a process failure: a request channel that produces data for months without triggering a second look. The 680-account targeting suggests the attackers knew what they wanted before they started asking.

Research

FamousSparrow's new SparroWocky backdoor extends China-aligned espionage across multiple Latin American countries, with activity tracked for more than a year China-Aligned FamousSparrow Deploys SparroWocky Backdoor Across Latin America. It's a modular C++ implant, which matters less for detection than for tempo: modular tradecraft means the operators can swap loaders and capabilities without re-registering infrastructure signatures, so signature-based hunting ages badly. If you have Latin American operations, subsidiaries or partners, this is a region worth adding to your collection priorities.

Industry

The seizure of NightmareStresser's two domains disrupts a DDoS-for-hire operation tied to hundreds of thousands of attacks U.S. Seizes NightmareStresser Domains Linked to Hundreds of Thousands of DDoS Attacks. Seizures of this kind are worth noting for what they do and don't do: they remove a storefront and its payment flow, inconvenience the operators and generate usable intelligence, but the customer base and capability persist and migrate to the next booter within weeks. The durable effect is the pressure on the operators, not a reduction in attack volume.
